My Story

I didn't start out in AI. My first degree was in Civil Engineering, back home in Lahore — a world of load calculations and concrete, not neural networks. But somewhere along the way, I found myself far more curious about the "why" behind decisions than the decisions themselves, and that curiosity is what eventually pulled me toward artificial intelligence.

Today I'm pursuing my Master's in AI at Woosong University, and that shift in direction has turned into the most demanding, rewarding work I've done. I've trained a CNN to diagnose mechanical faults from sound alone, built a medical question-answering system that cites its sources, and I'm now helping lead a federated learning project across multiple universities — all while keeping a perfect 4.0 GPA and, somehow, my sanity.

Living and studying in Korea has also meant learning to move fluidly between English and Korean, and between very different academic cultures. I think that's shaped how I communicate almost as much as my research has shaped how I think.

I care less about models that are accurate, and more about models people can actually trust.

What I'm Working On Right Now

Explainable AI

A CNN that listens to machinery and flags faults before they become failures — paired with SHAP and LIME so engineers can see exactly why it made a call. Currently under review at IJEEI.

Medical NLP

A retrieval-augmented question-answering system that answers health questions with real, cited sources — built to push back against the flood of inaccurate health info online.

Federated Learning

Leading a multi-university effort to predict depression risk from psychometric data — without ever moving anyone's private data off their own campus.
